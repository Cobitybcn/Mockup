artwork: look at it. Reply with strict JSON only, no prose outside the JSON.

Each field has a different job and must NOT reuse the others' wording:
  title      identifies the work and names its dominant visual trait

STEP 1 — LOOK AT THE ARTWORK FIRST
Register internally, from the image: dominant and secondary colours, composition type, main
shapes, how many relevant forms, line directions, spatial structure, surface qualities, visible
texture, contrast level, visual movement, mood, the distinctive feature, and any landscape or
architectural reference.

Separate three kinds of statement and never promote one to another:
  VISIBLE   colour, count of forms, lines, slopes, divisions, texture, contrast, relative
            position, colour fields, apparent depth. You may assert these directly.
  CONFIRMED technique, materials, dimensions, support, series, year, artist intent, symbolic
            meaning, influences. ONLY if given below as confirmed data.
  INFERRED  atmosphere, stillness, tension, balance, visual movement, sense of distance. Use
            sparingly. Never turn an inference into biography, narrative or symbol.

STEP 2 — TITLE
Format: official title, then " · ", then a visual subtitle.
The subtitle names what visually HAPPENS in this specific work: a relation between forms, lines,
space, colour, direction, void, surface, light, or figure and ground.
Shape it as: visible element + relation + spatial condition.
Good: "Four Blocks Along a Diagonal" · "Red Forms Across a Dark Field" · "A Bright Opening Within Deep Blue"
The subtitle must NOT repeat the title or series, name the artist, name a category, technique,
materials or dimensions, use "artwork", "painting" or "wall art", or interpret unconfirmed symbols.
Reject semantic contradictions: "suspended blocks anchoring", "silent dramatic energy",
"static movement". Sentence case. The full title must fit the character budget given below.

STEP 3 — KEYWORDS
Generate 25 to 40 candidates, score each, then select. Do NOT keep the first twelve you thought of.

Select 12 with spread: 3 colours, 2-3 moods, 4-5 visible traits, 1-2 aesthetic affinities, and

Form: ONE or TWO words. Each entry 2 to 20 characters — this is a hard limit of Saatchi's upload

Rough Texture · White Lines · Divided Space · Architectural · Monochrome

STEP 4 — DESCRIPTION (English)
Between 800 and 1000 CHARACTERS. The 1000 is a hard limit of Saatchi's form; the 800 is a floor,
because this is the only space where the work is sold in words and stopping at 500 wastes half of
it. Count the characters before answering and expand the weakest movement if you fall short — by
adding observation, never by padding with adjectives. Three movements:
  1. what is seen: colour field, forms, lines, divisions, directions, contrast, spatial distribution
  2. materiality, using CONFIRMED data only: layers, glazes, impasto, incisions, scrapes, marks,
     how the surface behaves
  3. conceptual reading — territory, memory, presence, transit, distance, structure, tension,
     horizon — only where it genuinely belongs to this work or its series
Lead with the strongest concrete observation: a buyer reads the first line and stops.
It must carry at least three traits exclusive to this work: an exact count of forms, the dominant
direction, a chromatic contrast, the position of elements, the specific kind of division, or a
particularity of the surface. Never restate measurements: they are published as their own field.
Forbidden: masterpiece, museum-quality, pivotal work, important work, highly collectible,
investment artwork, statement piece, commands attention, perfect for, ideal for, transforms any
room, luxury interiors. No opening command (Discover, Explore, Acquire).
Do not pile adjectives (deep, rich, vibrant, powerful, dramatic, tactile, intense) and do not use
one conceptual root more than twice (tension / tense / visual tension).

Good: "Full view of the artwork" · "Detail of the incised white lines" · "Side view showing the

The camera name orients you but is never copied verbatim.
When the image is a generated scene rather than real documentation, describe the VIEW and never

nothing untrue and needs no disclaimer.
Captions must not interpret the work, repeat the full title, name the artist, stack keywords, use
promotional language, explain emotions, describe the camera, or repeat each other.
Avoid: "Dramatic upward view emphasizing presence", "Low perspective revealing structural tension".
