GOAL
The artwork's reading is written and approved. You are not analysing the work again and you are not
looking at it: you are carrying that same reading into Saatchi's containers, in English, keeping one
voice across the site and the listing. Every visual, material or conceptual fact you state must
already be present in the approved reading or in the confirmed data below. Do not add an
observation, a colour, a count or a material that is not there — you cannot see the artwork, and an
invented detail would contradict the page that is already published.

SOURCE AUTHORITY
- The approved reading is the source of the voice and of every visual and conceptual claim.
- Facts in CONFIRMED ARTWORK DATA are confirmed artist/record facts.
- The artwork title is fixed by the artist: never produce, propose, translate or vary it.

RULES CARRIED FROM THE SYSTEM PROMPT (unchanged)

SAATCHI DESCRIPTION (English)
Target between 450 and 700 characters (hard limit: 1000 characters). Write a focused, agile, collector-oriented description without padding or repetitive curatorial fillers.
Vary your narrative entry point based on what truly distinguishes this specific artwork (e.g. open by its tactile materiality, its geometric division, its tonal movement, or its spatial tension). Do not repeat identical 3-part paragraph templates across different artworks.
Avoid repetitive filler phrases such as "contemplative stillness", "state of suspension", or "territorial blocks" unless strictly necessary.
Rewrite the approved reading concisely into this budget, keeping its register — the way it moves from what is visible to what the work makes present.
Open by making the work appear, not by classifying it. Anchor that opening in concrete evidence. Close on what the experience leaves.
Material construction only from confirmed data, and say how the process affects the visible surface rather than listing media.


SAATCHI KEYWORDS
Return exactly 12 phrases in English, each 2 to 20 characters including spaces, Title Case.
Build a balanced set of search entrances: what the buyer sees, what the buyer wants to feel, and
which authorised artistic universe may lead them toward the work.
  WHAT THEY SEE    colour, palette, visible elements, surface condition, composition
                   e.g. Burgundy Tones · Fine Incisions · Isolated Red Forms · Divided Dark Field
  WHAT THEY FEEL   the emotional promise of the work, when the reading sustains it
                   e.g. Quiet Solemnity · Muted Mystery · Distant Presences · Restrained Intensity
  A UNIVERSE THEY  authorised affinity names only, at most two
  ALREADY KNOW     e.g. Barnett Newman · Nicolas de Stael
Not every keyword describes an object: some name the emotional promise of the work, which is part of
why anyone buys art. The examples are structural — use them only if the reading sustains them.
Phrases normally run two to four words. Never add a generic filler word to reach length — "Deep Red",
not "Deep Red Art". Never use Art, Artwork, Painting, Abstract, Modern, Contemporary, Original,

Do not repeat meaningful words from the full Saatchi title or the artist name — singular, plural and

AUTHORISED ARTIST AFFINITIES
Each authorised name comes with the artist's own account of what they take from that painter. That
account is the criterion: it names a concrete visual operation, and you select the affinity only
when the approved reading shows this particular work performing it. Do not pick the same names for
every artwork out of habit — pick the ones this work earns.
Affinity is not limited to direct formal resemblance. Weigh each on three dimensions: visual
affinity (colour fields, forms, scale, spatial division, material presence); emotional affinity
(stillness, solemnity, austerity, distance, immersion, restrained intensity, monumental presence);
and buyer expectation — the experience someone is seeking when they type that name. Select one when
the visual connection is credible, the emotional experience is related, and the association would
not mislead a buyer about the character of the work. Direct historical influence is not required.
Never call the artist an influence unless confirmed, never write "inspired by", "in the style of" or
"like", never place the name in the title or the description, and never select more than two. Let
the mood keywords and the affinity names reinforce one another instead of working as unrelated tags.
